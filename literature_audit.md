## Manuscript verdict and compact recommendations

The abstract, Sections 1 and 9, and existing source descriptions agree with the recorded audit. The final historical extensions and their additional classical-method comparisons are recorded separately in extension_novelty_audit.md. The source-specific startup assumptions appear in Appendices A–B; the original single-peaked theorem remains valid. The draft distinguishes first loss of admissibility from pulse existence/propagation, separates exact finite-radius results from inner-limit results, credits the decisive Viesca formula, and states Gaussian nucleation and source geometry limitations. No originality overclaim requiring retraction was found in those passages.

The following three recommendations from the audit have been incorporated in the final manuscript without widening the contribution:

1. Cite Jacquey–Viesca alongside the 2023 circular post-injection work as a closely related numerical antecedent with different geometry and source boundary conditions.
2. Cite Ji et al. for the operational caveat. Suggested sentence: “Recent experimental and modelling comparisons of shut-in strategies show that their effects depend on hydraulic connectivity, fault geometry and poroelastic stress; the present admissibility criterion therefore does not identify a safest operating schedule.” This should not be read as a recommendation to extract or continue injection.
3. Cite Passelègue et al. (2026) for current experimental context, while saying that their experiments concern propagation under rising pressure and do not test the proposed locking criterion.

The arcsine continuation and linear-taper formula are best presented exactly as now: explicit consequences of the limiting criterion and classical Abel inversion, not independently new special-function identities or universal operational optima. A final targeted search used `"arcsin" "injection" "aseismic"`, `"arcsine" "taper" injection`, `"minimum" "continuation" "half derivative"`, and `"arcsin" "positive" "Abel" continuation`. No geological predecessor was retrieved, but negative search results cannot establish priority.

This screening is not a full-text clearance of those papers.

Thus “no exact predecessor was found within the inspected boundary” remains the appropriate statement. No author was contacted, and no claim of exhaustive literature coverage is justified.
